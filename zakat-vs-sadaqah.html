Zakat vs sadaqah: what’s the difference?

Both mean giving for the sake of Allah, but the rules differ. What is obligatory, what is voluntary, and who receives each.

Yrys guide · 5 min read · updated 18 November 2026

In short. Zakat is obligatory: 2.5% of your savings once a year, if they reach the nisab. Sadaqah is voluntary charity: as much as you like, whenever you like, to anyone in need. Zakat is a form of sadaqah too, but an obligatory one with its own rules.

Who receives zakat: the eight categories

The recipients of zakat are named in the Quran (Surah at-Tawbah, 9:60):

  1. The poor (fuqara), who have almost nothing.
  2. The needy (masakin), who have some income but not enough for their basic needs.
  3. Those who collect and distribute zakat.
  4. Those whose hearts are to be won over.
  5. Freeing slaves and captives.
  6. Debtors who cannot repay a debt taken for something permissible.
  7. In the cause of Allah.
  8. Travellers stranded without means.

Most zakat goes to the first two categories and to debtors. You can give directly to someone you know, or through a mosque or charity you trust.

Who does not receive zakat

  • Your parents, grandparents, children and grandchildren, and your husband or wife, whom you support anyway. Brothers, sisters, uncles and aunts in need may receive it, and it is even better: zakat and family ties together.
  • Someone who has the nisab themselves.
  • Most scholars hold that zakat is given to Muslims. Sadaqah can be given to anyone.

Zakat al-fitr: a third kind

Also called sadaqat al-fitr or fitrana. It is neither the yearly zakat nor voluntary sadaqah:

  • Obligatory for every Muslim who has more than their basic needs on the day of Eid. You give it for yourself and for those you support, such as your children.
  • When: at the end of Ramadan, before the Eid al-Fitr prayer; it can also be given a few days earlier. In 2027 Eid is expected around 9–10 March.
  • How much: one sa‘ (about 2.5–3 kg) of a staple food per person, or its value in money where your school allows it. Many mosques and national bodies announce the amount each year.
  • To whom: the poor and needy, so that they can celebrate Eid too.

Common questions

I gave sadaqah all year. Can I count it as zakat?

No. Zakat only counts if you intended it as zakat when you gave it. The sadaqah you already gave stays sadaqah, and its reward is yours. Work out your zakat separately.

Do I have to tell the person it is zakat?

No. Your intention is what matters. Many people give zakat as a gift so as not to embarrass the person.

Can I give zakat in instalments during the year?

Yes. You can set some aside little by little and give it when you see a need, as long as it is all given by your zakat date. Giving early is also allowed if you already have the nisab.

Can bank interest be given as zakat or sadaqah?

No. Interest (riba) is not your money: it is given away to the needy without intending reward, simply to be rid of it. It becomes neither zakat nor sadaqah.

How much sadaqah should I give?

As much as you can without harming your family. The Prophet ﷺ said that charity does not decrease wealth (Sahih Muslim), and that the deeds most beloved to Allah are the regular ones, even if small. That is why many people give sadaqah its own line in the budget; see the guide “Halal budget”.

This is a general overview. The schools differ in the details, so for your own situation ask a scholar you trust.
