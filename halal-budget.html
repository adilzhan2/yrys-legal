Halal budget: six simple steps

How to run family money so that there is no riba in it, the essentials are covered, and sadaqah and zakat are planned, not “if anything is left”.

Yrys guide · 6 min read · updated 18 November 2026

A halal budget is an ordinary family budget in which money is earned and spent in permissible ways: without riba (interest), without waste, with sadaqah and zakat in the plan. Islam is not against prosperity; it asks that money be clean and serve good.

Three principles behind it

  • No riba. Allah has forbidden riba, interest on a debt (Surah al-Baqarah, 2:275). This applies both to paying interest and to receiving it.
  • The middle way. The Quran praises those who, when they spend, are neither wasteful nor stingy, but keep a balance between the two (Surah al-Furqan, 25:67).
  • Write it down. The longest verse of the Quran (2:282) is about recording debts. Keeping track of money is part of being responsible for it.

Step 1. Work out what comes in

Add up everything the household receives in a month: salaries, business income, benefits. If your income varies, use the average of the last 3–6 months, or the lowest month, so you never overestimate.

Check the sources too: income should be halal. If bank interest has slipped in, don’t count it as yours; it is given away to the needy without intending reward.

Step 2. Sadaqah first

Sadaqah usually comes from whatever is left over, and usually nothing is. Do it the other way round: make it the first line. Give as much as you can without harming your family; many people choose a fixed share, for example 2–5% of income. The Prophet ﷺ said that charity does not decrease wealth (Sahih Muslim).

Step 3. Essentials

What you cannot do without: rent or housing costs and bills, food, transport, healthcare, children’s education, repaying debts. Write each one down separately and honestly, based on what you actually spent in past months, not what you think it should be.

Step 4. Savings, without interest

An emergency fund covering 3–6 months of essentials is a good target. Keep it in an account that pays no interest, in cash or in gold. For big purchases (a phone, a car, furniture) it is better to save up than to borrow with interest.

Once your savings reach the nisab, zakat will be due on them after a lunar year, which is why zakat is also in the plan in step 6.

Step 5. The rest is for living, without waste

Clothes, gifts, holidays, eating out and hobbies are not sins; they are part of normal life. What matters is spending within your means and without extravagance: the Quran says eat and drink, but do not waste, for Allah does not love the wasteful (Surah al-A‘raf, 7:31). Give each category its own monthly limit.

Step 6. Zakat in the yearly plan

Choose a zakat date (many choose Ramadan) and once a year work out zakat on all your savings: cash, gold, silver and business goods minus debts. If you know roughly how much it will be in advance, it is easier to give without strain.

A budget for Ramadan

Ramadan 2027 is expected to begin around 8 February. Spending changes during the month, so plan it in advance, in January:

  • Iftars. You buy more food, especially when inviting guests. Give it its own limit.
  • Zakat al-fitr for each member of the family, before the Eid prayer.
  • Zakat, if you give it in Ramadan.
  • Sadaqah: many give more than usual in Ramadan.
  • Eid al-Fitr: gifts, food, new clothes for the children.

For the difference between these payments, see the guide “Zakat vs sadaqah”.

What to do about debts

  • Write down every debt: to whom, how much and when it is due (2:282).
  • Repay on time. Repaying debts is an essential, before wants.
  • If you already have an interest-bearing loan, make a plan to clear it as soon as possible, and don’t take on new ones.
  • If a purchase is necessary and you don’t have the money, it is better to wait and save. If an instalment plan can’t be avoided, it must have no interest and no late-payment penalties; check the terms with a scholar.

This is a general approach, not a fatwa. For your own situation (income, debts, contracts), ask a scholar you trust.
