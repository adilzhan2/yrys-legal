Zakat Calculator

Three simple steps to find out whether you owe zakat, and how much. Skip any field that doesn’t apply to you.

Zakat is an obligatory charity: once a year a Muslim gives 2.5% of their savings to people in need. It is due only if your savings reach the nisab, the value of 85 g of gold or 595 g of silver.
1Nisab: gold or silver

The nisab is the minimum amount on which zakat is due. It is set by the price of gold or silver. Not sure which to pick? Leave gold.

All four schools accept both thresholds for gold and silver themselves. The difference is how to count money: the Hanafi school takes the threshold that is better for the poor, silver; many contemporary scholars (Yusuf al-Qaradawi, the AAOIFI standard) use gold, because silver has lost much of its value.

3Debts, loans and instalments
A regular debt: the full amount you have to repay. A loan or instalment plan: only the next 12 payments, including this month’s if you haven’t paid it yet; if fewer are left, just those. Not the whole balance. For example, 500 a month → 6,000.

Why 12 months. This is the view of many contemporary scholars (Taqi Usmani, the AAOIFI standard): a long loan is repaid bit by bit, and subtracting all of it would mean zakat is almost never due. Classically, the Hanafi and Hanbali schools subtract the whole debt, and the Shafi’i school holds that debt doesn’t reduce zakat. If you follow one of these views, enter the amount by it.

How zakat is calculated

  1. Add up your zakatable wealth: cash, bank balances, savings, gold, silver, investments, business goods and money owed to you.
  2. Subtract your debts: debts and bills you have to repay, and for loans and instalment plans the payments for the next 12 months.
  3. Compare with the nisab: the value of 85 g of gold or 595 g of silver.
  4. If you are at or above the nisab for one lunar year, pay 2.5% of the total.

Example: you have 4,000 in the bank, 1,500 in cash and 20 g of gold at 80 per gram (1,600), and owe 600 in bills now. Your zakatable wealth is 4,000 + 1,500 + 1,600 − 600 = 6,500. If silver is 1 per gram, the nisab is 595, so zakat is due: 6,500 × 2.5% = 162.50.

What is the nisab?

The nisab is the minimum amount of wealth you must have before zakat is due. It is set by the price of gold or silver:

BasisWeightNisab in your currency
Gold85 g (about 2.73 troy oz)85 × gold price per gram
Silver595 g (about 19.13 troy oz)595 × silver price per gram

Because silver is much cheaper than gold, the silver nisab is much lower. Many scholars recommend it for cash and mixed wealth so that more people give. Others use gold. Choose the one your school or scholar follows.

The lunar year (hawl)

Zakat is due once your wealth has stayed at or above the nisab for one lunar year, about 354 days. Many people pick a fixed date, often in Ramadan, and pay every year on that date.

What is not zakatable

  • Your home, car, furniture, clothes and other things you use.
  • Tools and equipment you work with.
  • Property you live in or rent out (the rent you have saved is zakatable).

Common questions

How much is zakat?

Zakat is 2.5% (one fortieth) of your zakatable wealth, once it has stayed at or above the nisab for one full lunar year.

Should I use the gold or the silver nisab?

Many scholars recommend the silver nisab for cash and mixed wealth because it is lower, so more people give and the poor benefit more. Others use gold. Follow the school or scholar you trust.

Do I pay zakat on my house or car?

No. Personal items you use, such as your home, car, furniture and clothes, are not zakatable. Property or goods held for sale are.

Can I subtract debts from zakat?

Debts and bills due now are usually subtracted before checking the nisab. For long-term loans, many scholars subtract only the instalments due in the coming year.

Do I pay zakat on gold jewellery I wear?

Scholars differ. The Hanafi school counts worn jewellery; many others do not. The calculator counts whatever gold you enter, so leave worn jewellery out if your school exempts it.

Is my data sent anywhere?

No. The calculator runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you type is stored or sent to a server.
