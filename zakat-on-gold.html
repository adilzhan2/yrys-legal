How to calculate zakat on gold

Four steps: find the pure weight, compare it with the nisab, multiply by today’s price, take 2.5%. Each step below, with an example.

Yrys guide · 5 min read · updated 18 November 2026

In short. Zakat on gold is due if you own 85 g of pure gold or more (the nisab) and have held it for a full lunar year. You give 2.5%, one fortieth, either in gold or in money at today’s price.

Step 1. Find the pure weight

Zakat is worked out on pure gold only. The karat tells you how much of an item is gold: 24K is pure, 22K is 22 parts gold out of 24. So:

Pure gold = item weight × karat ÷ 24

Stones (diamonds, pearls and so on) don’t count: if you know their weight, subtract it. The weight and karat are usually on the receipt or tag; if not, any jeweller can tell you.

Step 2. Compare with the nisab: 85 g

The nisab for gold is 85 grams of pure gold (20 mithqal). Add up the pure weight of all your jewellery, coins and bars.

  • 85 g or more: zakat is due, if you have held it for a full lunar year (about 354 days).
  • Less than 85 g: no zakat on the gold alone. But if you also have cash or silver, they are added together by value; if the total reaches the nisab, zakat is due on all of it. This is the view of the Hanafi school and many contemporary scholars, and it is how our calculator works.

Step 3. Multiply by today’s price

Use today’s market price per gram of pure gold on the day you work out your zakat. Not what you paid, and not a shop price that includes the jeweller’s workmanship.

Value of gold = pure weight × price per gram today

Step 4. Take 2.5%

Zakat = value of gold × 0.025

You can give zakat in gold itself (2.5% of the weight) or in money. Most people give money: it is simpler, and more useful to the person receiving it.

A worked example

Aisha has 22K bangles and a necklace weighing 60 g in total, and a 1 oz gold coin (31.1 g, 999.9). She has owned them for over a year. On 18 November 2026, pure gold was about $122.50 per gram.

Jewellery: 60 g × 22 ÷ 24≈ 54.96 g
Coin: 31.1 g × 999.9 ÷ 1000≈ 31.1 g
Total pure gold≈ 86.06 g, at or above the nisab
Value: 86.06 g × $122.50≈ $10,545
Zakat: 2.5%≈ $264 (or ≈ 2.15 g of gold)

If Aisha follows a school in which worn jewellery is exempt (see below), she counts only the coin: 31.1 g is under the nisab, so unless she has other savings, no zakat is due this year.

Jewellery you wear: zakat or not?

This is where the schools differ, and both views rest on hadith:

  • Hanafi school (most Muslims in South Asia, Turkey and Central Asia): zakat is due on all gold, including jewellery you wear. Some contemporary scholars of other schools, such as Ibn Baz and Ibn Uthaymeen, held the same.
  • Maliki, Shafi‘i and Hanbali schools: no zakat on jewellery a woman wears in a normal amount for her. If it is kept as savings, or far exceeds what is usual, zakat is due.

In every view, zakat is due on bars, coins and gold bought as an investment. If you are unsure, many scholars advise paying on jewellery too, to be on the safe side.

Common questions

Who pays zakat on a wife’s jewellery?

The owner of the gold, the wife. Her husband may pay it for her from his own money, but only with her consent.

I received gold as a gift or inheritance six months ago. Do I pay this year?

If you already had the nisab and pay zakat on one date each year, the new gold is counted with everything else on that date; it doesn’t need its own year (the Hanafi view). If you didn’t have the nisab before, your year starts when you reached it.

Gold held in an account or as “digital gold”

If you own a specific number of grams, count them as gold using the same formula. It is worth checking the account itself and its terms with a scholar first.

When do I pay?

Once a lunar year, on the same date. Many choose Ramadan; in 2027 it is expected to begin around 8 February. You may also pay a little early if you already have the nisab: most scholars allow it.

This is a guide for working it out. Rulings differ between schools, so for your own situation ask a scholar you trust.
